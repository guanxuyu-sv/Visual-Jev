One image. Many questions. The win is in the execution.

Software asks a vision–language model for a decision about an image rather than for prose, and usually asks several questions at once. We post-train a 4B backbone for that interface and measure both halves of it — what the training buys, and what the serving path buys. One of those answers is a zero, and it is the more useful half.

The workload

Ask once, answer many

An instance is one image, one public text context, and a set of questions defined at request time. Each question is answered independently — it may read the image, the context and its own text, and never the other questions or their answers. That isolation is what licenses everything downstream: the image and the shared prefix are computed once, and the question branches run as one batch.

Two output types. Choice returns a distribution over a candidate set supplied with the request. Claim returns supported / contradicted / not-determined. Four benchmarks, two of which post-training never sees.

Axis one

What training buys

Post-training for this interface lifts the equal-weight macro average over four benchmarks from __M_B1__ to __M_B2K__. The gain is concentrated on the two families in the training mix; on the two held-out tasks it is small.

What does not show up is any contribution from the decision head. Answer SFT — same items, same steps, same prompts, same readout position, ordinary next-token cross entropy through the LM head — reaches __M_B2K__. Three seeds each, ranges overlapping completely.

Axis two

What the serving path buys

Comparing a shared, batched path against an independent, serial one conflates two mechanisms. Cross them and both turn out to be real — and their product, not either one, is the headline.

Two things that surprised us. Emitting an answer token costs nothing against reading a head once batching and sharing are matched (__MS_GEN__ ms against __MS_SHARED__ ms), so the advantage is the shared prefix, not the absence of a decode. And at N=1 sharing is a loss (__MS_N1_SHARED__ ms against __MS_N1_IND__ ms) — the gain is a property of the workload, not of the method.

Against the ordinary way

What this replaces

Today you would ask a vision–language model these questions by prompting it and reading what it writes — either one request per question, or all of them in one prompt and let it answer in a list. We measured both, on the same GPU, same model, same images and same questions.

Two honest caveats. The gap is a property of this workload — at N=1 there is no shared context to amortise and the ordinary way wins. And putting every question in one prompt lets them see each other, which is a different task: the answers can condition on one another, for better or worse.

On quality the comparable baseline is an off-the-shelf backbone asked to pick among the same candidates: __M_B1__ macro against __M_B2K__ after post-training. We did not score the generative paths for accuracy — parsing free text into a choice is a different measurement, and reporting it beside ours would flatter us for their formatting errors.

The appendix, visibly

Confident without the evidence

Each row is one judgement under three conditions: the original image, the same image with the region the question depends on destroyed, and a control with an equal-area region elsewhere destroyed. All three were chosen because the model answers correctly with the evidence and incorrectly without it.

Watch the two signals diverge. Confidence barely moves even where the answer becomes wrong — it is still above 0.6 on every one of these. The sufficiency output, trained on these pairs, collapses instead.

It is not a clean instrument, and the rows show that rather than hide it: on the first, sufficiency also drops on the control, where the question's evidence is untouched. The paper measures how often that happens — the drop on the relevant arm is about five times the drop on the control, not infinitely more.

Reported anyway

What didn't work

Four things we built or expected that the measurements did not support. They are here because each one changes what a reader should do next.

__HEAD__

The decision head

Against answer SFT at matched data and budget, on four benchmarks and three seeds. The interface is worth building; the head on top of it is not what makes it work.

__SUFF__

Evidence-sufficiency objectives

The output detects removed evidence far better than confidence does — and still costs macro accuracy. Knowing evidence is missing is not knowing an answer is wrong.

__SCALE__

Twice the parameters

8B against 4B, same recipe and GPU, for +__SCALE_LAT__% latency and +__SCALE_MEM__% memory. Barely outside seed noise, and an order of magnitude less than the serving path.

__SLOT__

A trap, not a result

Train a slot-indexed head only on K≤4 and slots 4+ never get a gradient. The held-out benchmark then reads as a collapse in generalisation. It isn't one — it is the data.
